Safety events rarely present themselves as a single clean signal. Driver fatigue may appear through facial behaviour, voice, posture, and environmental context. An early-stage fire can produce weak and partially conflicting visual, thermal, gas, and flame cues.

Multimodal systems can improve robustness, but adding sensors is not enough. The central engineering questions are when each modality is trustworthy, how uncertainty propagates through fusion, and what should happen when a sensor becomes unavailable.

My work on driver monitoring and FireGuard has motivated a broader research interest in safety-critical perception: building edge systems that combine heterogeneous signals while remaining efficient, interpretable, and deployable.
